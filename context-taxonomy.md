# Taxonomy of Censorship
## Operational Families, Strategies, and Descriptive Dimensions

This document is the canonical compact taxonomy for the narrowed essay. `outline.md` is authoritative for argument order, boundary conditions, and emphasis.

## 1. Taxonomic principle

The taxonomy classifies censorship by **what happens to participation in an epistemic environment**, not by who performs the intervention, why it is justified, or whether it is morally accepted.

The taxonomy distinguishes four levels that must not be collapsed:

1. **Operational families** — primary kinds of censorial operation at a common level of abstraction.
2. **Subtypes** — useful recurring forms within a family.
3. **Boundary-sensitive strategies or practices** — things such as delegitimation, inversion, contradiction, selection, curation, or ranking whose censorial significance depends on the operation they produce in context.
4. **Descriptive dimensions** — scale, power, participation, persistence, opacity, justification, and control surface/domain.

A state, platform, institution, employer, library, journal, community, technical system, or diffuse norm can instantiate the same operational family.

## 2. Boundary condition

Not every influence on belief, attention, reputation, access, or selection is censorship.

The framework distinguishes two ideal types:

- **epistemic contest** — changing belief or judgment principally through reasons, evidence, interpretation, criticism, rebuttal, comparison, or argument within an existing field of participation;
- **epistemic control** — changing the practical conditions under which participation in that field can occur.

These are not categorical opposites in practice. Many institutional acts combine both.

Selection is unavoidable in finite epistemic environments. A librarian cannot acquire every book; an editor cannot publish every submission; a search system cannot rank every result first. Selection is therefore neither sufficient to establish censorship nor sufficient to exclude it.

Boundary analysis considers:

- differential treatment;
- control of a consequential resource or channel;
- whether disadvantage operates through participation conditions rather than solely through merits contest;
- relation to a preferred epistemic ordering;
- scarcity, institutional remit, reach, substitutability, alternatives, pattern, opacity, and power.

No single factor produces an automatic verdict. Borderline cases should remain borderline where warranted.

## 3. Operational families

### A. Exclusion

Censorial control removes or prevents participation.

1. **Prevention**  
   Blocking expression, publication, acquisition, access, or entry before it occurs.

2. **Erasure**  
   Removing existing expression, records, traces, access, or recognised standing after it exists.

Prevention and erasure are direct subtypes of the same exclusion family.

### B. Attenuation

Censorial control leaves the target nominally present while reducing practical access, reach, timeliness, discoverability, persistence, or effective audience.

Useful subtypes include:

1. **Demotion / suppression**  
   Reducing ranking, reach, recommendation, discoverability, circulation, or persistence.

2. **Friction / delay**  
   Raising the time, effort, expertise, cost, or delay required to encounter, access, or use the target.

3. **Containment**  
   Confining expression to bounded, marginal, specialised, age-gated, jurisdiction-limited, or otherwise low-reach spaces.

These are not treated as peer natural kinds because their common operation is attenuation.

### C. Transformation

Censorial control changes what is available or represented.

1. **Modification**  
   Altering content, framing, metadata, translation, redaction, classification, or representation while retaining some continuity with an original.

2. **Substitution**  
   Replacing a contested expression, account, framing, representation, or record with a preferred alternative.

Transformation can compose with exclusion or attenuation.

### D. Saturation / flooding

Censorial control changes the surrounding information environment so that a target becomes harder to find, attend to, distinguish, or use without being removed.

- Noise, repetition, diversion, coordinated competing material, or sheer information volume may create the suppressive effect.
- Ordinary contradiction and counter-speech are not saturation merely because they compete for attention.
- Counter-assertion becomes relevant here when scale, coordination, or environmental effect changes the practical conditions of encounter.

### E. Induced self-censorship

Expected consequences alter future expression before an external censor must act again.

- Anticipatory restraint may be produced by sanctions, incentives, norms, prior enforcement, opaque rules, or learned expectations.
- The proximate act is performed by the target, but the censorial condition is externally or socially reproduced.
- Ordinary private reconsideration, uncertainty, politeness, or a change of mind is insufficient by itself.

## 4. Boundary-sensitive strategies and practices

These concepts can be analytically important without being primary operational families.

### 4.1 Delegitimation

Attacking credibility, competence, motive, status, or entitlement to be heard.

- It can be ordinary epistemic contest when credibility or standing is itself disputed on the merits.
- It becomes censorially relevant when it functions to produce exclusion, attenuation, or induced self-censorship through loss of practical standing or access.

### 4.2 Inversion

Reframing an expression so that the act of expressing it becomes evidence of harm, threat, pathology, disloyalty, extremism, or another disqualifying status.

- The reframing itself is not automatically censorship.
- Its censorial significance lies in whether it changes admissibility, standing, reach, access, or anticipated consequences.

### 4.3 Contradiction

Counter-assertion or rebuttal.

- Ordinary contradiction is epistemic contest and is not a primary censorial operation.
- Coordinated or scaled contradiction that occupies the environment and suppresses practical encounter is classified by its operative effect, usually saturation or attenuation.

### 4.4 Selection and curation

Selection, editing, acquisition, moderation, ranking, and curation are not separate operational families and are not categorically outside censorship.

- A selection decision can instantiate exclusion, attenuation, transformation, or no censorial operation at all.
- Institutional title or professional role does not decide classification.
- Scarcity and remit matter but do not automatically immunise a practice from censorship analysis.

## 5. Taxonomy caveats

- The taxonomy is a proposed analytic vocabulary, not a claim to have discovered natural kinds.
- Families may overlap and compose.
- Subtypes can be refined without multiplying primary families unnecessarily.
- Borderline practices should be analysed through function and context rather than forced into a binary label.
- If recurring empirical cases do not fit, revise the taxonomy rather than force-fit them.
- The five-family structure is deliberately smaller than the earlier twelve-mode list because the earlier list mixed operations, subtypes, strategies, and responses.

## 6. Descriptive dimensions

The following describe **how and where** an operation occurs. They are analytically separable but are not claimed to be mathematically orthogonal, statistically independent, or causally unrelated.

### 6.1 Scale

- individual;
- group/community;
- institutional;
- national;
- transnational/platform-scale.

### 6.2 Power and channel control

- diffuse or majoritarian;
- concentrated institutional;
- elite minority;
- economic;
- technical/algorithmic;
- mixed or distributed.

Relevant questions include who controls a consequential epistemic resource and how substitutable that resource is.

### 6.3 Participation in preference formation

- broad participation;
- limited participation;
- imposed preference;
- unclear, inherited, or emergent formation.

Participation is descriptive in this essay and does not establish a general theory of legitimacy.

### 6.4 Persistence and revisability

- one-off or temporary;
- recurrent;
- persistent;
- entrenched/infrastructural.

A related question is how readily the relevant rule, ranking, classification, norm, or settlement can be reopened and changed.

### 6.5 Visibility / opacity of the mechanism

- overt;
- implicit;
- opaque;
- only indirectly inferable.

This concerns visibility of the censorial mechanism, not visibility of the target.

### 6.6 Justification regime

- moral/harm;
- legal/rights;
- safety/risk;
- professional/epistemic quality;
- technical/efficiency;
- economic/market;
- procedural/neutrality.

Justification is analytically separate from mechanism. A justification can be sincere without determining classification.

### 6.7 Control surface / domain

- availability/access;
- circulation/distribution;
- timing;
- visibility/ranking/attention;
- legibility/classification;
- standing/admissibility;
- memory/archive.

Several surfaces may be affected at once.

## 7. Classification sequence

For an illustrative case:

1. Identify the relevant epistemic preference if one can be inferred without pretending certainty.
2. Identify the control surface or consequential resource at issue.
3. Ask whether the intervention primarily contests a claim, changes participation conditions, or combines both.
4. Apply the boundary factors; preserve ambiguity where warranted.
5. Identify the operational family or families, then any useful subtype or strategy.
6. Describe only the contextual dimensions that materially aid analysis.
7. Only after structural classification, ask normative, legal, professional, political, or practical questions.

This sequence is intended to prevent both moral disapproval and professional labels from silently determining what counts as censorship.
