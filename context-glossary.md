# Concept Glossary
## Canonical Terms

- **Censorship**: mechanisms that enforce epistemic preference by altering the practical conditions under which expression, claims, records, or speakers can appear, circulate, persist, remain visible, or count as admissible.
- **Epistemic preference**: a preference about what is credible, legitimate, visible, memorable, admissible, or worth attending to within an epistemic environment.
- **Epistemic environment**: the practical conditions governing availability, circulation, visibility, standing, attention, legibility, and persistence for claims, expression, and records.
- **Epistemic contest**: disagreement operating within the field through criticism, rebuttal, persuasion, evidence, and argument on the merits.
- **Epistemic control**: intervention in the practical conditions of participation in the field, including availability, access, reach, persistence, visibility, admissibility, standing, attention, or memory.
- **Operational mode**: how censorial control is performed, independent of actor, scale, justification, or moral evaluation.
- **Analytic axis**: an orthogonal dimension describing conditions under which a censorial operation occurs without changing its operational kind.
- **Infrastructural censorship**: censorial operation embedded in ordinary rules, classifications, norms, incentives, technical systems, rankings, routines, archives, or anticipatory behaviour rather than appearing primarily as an exceptional prohibition.
- **Participation**: who takes part in forming or maintaining the relevant epistemic preference or settlement.
- **Persistence/revisability**: how durable a censorial arrangement is and how readily it can be reopened or changed.
- **Visibility**: how overt, implicit, opaque, or internalised the censorial mechanism is.
- **Justification regime**: the rationale offered for an intervention, such as harm, law, safety, professional quality, efficiency, market suitability, or procedural neutrality.
- **Domain of control**: the epistemic surface principally affected, such as expression, knowledge, attention, visibility, legibility, standing, or memory.
- **Composability**: the property that multiple censorial modes can overlap or operate together.
- **Shortcut**: a limited explanatory lens for mechanisms that reduce repeated deliberation or renegotiation by stabilising an epistemic preference; not a general theory of social control in this essay.
