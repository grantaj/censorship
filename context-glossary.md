# Concept Glossary
## Canonical Terms and One-Line Definitions

- **Censorship**: mechanisms that enforce epistemic preference by constraining expression or visibility, bypassing immediate debate.
- **Morality**: internalised norms that attract conduct toward preferred states, reducing renegotiation.
- **Conduct**: realised patterns of speech and behaviour; the regulated variable.
- **Epistemic Preference**: stabilised preferences about what counts as credible, legitimate, or real.
- **Shortcut Mechanism**: stability-seeking substitute for continuous deliberation under complexity.
- **Epistemic Control Model**: conduct regulated by censorship (negative feedback) and morality (positive feedback).
- **Operational Kind (of censorship)**: how control operates (e.g., erasure, suppression), independent of actors.
- **Analytic Axes**: orthogonal dimensions (scale, power, participation, temporal stability, visibility, justification, domain).
- **Justification Regime**: the rationale used to legitimise a mechanism (moral, legal, safety, technical, economic).
- **Legitimacy**: continuous measure of acceptance tied to participation and revisability.
- **Visibility**: degree to which control is overt, implicit, or internalised.
- **Participation**: who takes part in forming the epistemic preference or shortcut.
- **Revisability**: the system's capacity to re-open and revise a shortcut.
- **Infrastructural**: embedded, persistent, often invisible operation rather than exceptional events.
- **Epistemic Control**: the broader field of mechanisms shaping knowledge, attention, and legitimacy.
- **Composability**: mechanisms can layer and overlap rather than operate singly.
