# Censorship as Shortcut

## Global constraints

- Scope: censorship, not a general theory of morality, social control, political legitimacy, propaganda, persuasion, or social shortcuts.
- Descriptive first: identify censorial structure before evaluating justification, benefit, harm, proportionality, or legality.
- Cases are conceptual contrasts and boundary cases; sensitive cases are used because difficult boundaries are unusually visible, not as political advocacy or a historical survey.
- Theory discipline: retain only distinctions that clarify or compress the shortcut account.

### Source discipline

- Literature, empirical, historical, and attributed claims require verified support; hypothetical structural comparisons do not.
- Structural censorship and the idea that censorship can foreclose, frustrate, or short-circuit inquiry have clear antecedents; novelty is not claimed for those ideas by themselves.
- The authored contribution is the account developed here and the explanatory compression it produces; descriptive vocabulary is supporting coverage, not a second theory.

---

# 1. Positioning and Contribution

## Problem and lineage

- Censorship scholarship already extends beyond overt prohibition to structural or constitutive, self-censoring, frictional, and flooding forms; breadth beyond bans is not the novelty claim. Bourdieu shows how field structure can govern access to expression and expressive form, while Moore identifies the danger that sufficiently expansive structural accounts make ordinary conditions of communication difficult to distinguish from censorship. This is the demarcation problem: restriction or structuring of communicative possibility cannot by itself classify a relation as censorial. Support: `jansen1988`, `bourdieu1991`, `butler1998`, `muller2004`, `freshwater2004`, `bunn2015`, `moore2013`, `roberts2018`, `debaets2026`.
- A second lineage supplies the shortcut intuition. Steward argues that censorship can foreclose or frustrate inquiry, so apparent consensus may reflect a short-circuit of inquiry rather than successful contest. Atkinson makes the same structure visible in scientific gatekeeping, and Bachrach and Baratz show more generally how outcomes can be produced upstream by limiting what reaches decision. The present account generalises **inquiry** to **contest**: the processes through which practical questions of credibility, legitimacy, admissibility, visibility, recognition, authority, or other standing are established, defended, or changed. Contest need not be propositional argument; representation, artistic expression, self-presentation, attention, and challenges to standing can also be part of it. Support: `steward2014`, `atkinson1994`, `bachrachbaratz1962`, `bachrachbaratz1963`.
- A third lineage concerns participation. Democratic and content-moderation work uses `contestability` for practical openness to challenge; relational free-speech theory, illocutionary silencing, testimonial silencing, and argumentative exclusion all show that formal presence or permission to speak is not the same as effective participation. Anttila and Domínguez-Armas are the closest participation-side antecedent, but their object is argumentative exclusion; here the relevant contribution need not be an argument or even realised speech, and impaired participation is not sufficient for censorship. The further question is whether participation is controlled so as to bypass the contest through which the matter would otherwise be worked out. Support: `girard2015`, `trantidis2017`, `vaccaro2021`, `bonottiseglow2022`, `bishopforthcoming`, `hornsbylangton1998`, `dotson2011`, `young2000`, `anttiladominguezarmas2025`, `aikincasey2023`, `bejan2020`.
- Algorithmic and infrastructural scholarship extends these issues upstream. Cobbe emphasises the reach, interventionist capacity, private ordering, and structural power of algorithmic censorship; Elkin-Koren develops contestation as a governance response to AI filtering; Xin, Zheng, and Ni describe `discursive preclusion`, in which infrastructure conditions what becomes visible or expressible before post-hoc moderation. Xin, Zheng, and Ni are a particularly close upstream neighbour, but their own causal account remains diagnostic where proprietary mechanisms are not directly observed. Implementation, timing, or opacity does not by itself establish that contest has been bypassed. Support: `cobbe2021`, `elkinkoren2020`, `xinzhengni2026`.
- The account therefore occupies a middle position between overt-act theories and accounts on which constitutive constraint readily becomes censorship. Structural conditions can matter without all being censorial. The question is whether control of participation settles, prejudges, or forecloses something that would otherwise remain open to contest. Support: `bourdieu1991`, `butler1998`, `moore2013`.

## Proposed contribution

- Core proposal: **censorship is a shortcut around contest: it occurs where participation is controlled in a way that substitutes for, prejudges, or forecloses contest over something that would otherwise remain open**.
- The contribution is explanatory and demarcational: the same relation can illuminate overt, indirect, distributed, representational, self-censoring, upstream, and mechanised phenomena while preserving meaningful differences from ordinary criticism, selection, ranking, editing, moderation, organisation, and error.

# 2. Contest and Shortcut

## 2.1 Contest and participation

- **Contest** is the process through which the matter at issue is established, defended, or changed through reasons, evidence, interpretation, criticism, representation, comparison, response, or challenges to standing or admissibility. What is at issue must be specified in the terms of the case: scientific credibility, political legitimacy, visibility, historical interpretation, admissibility, or something else.
- Participation in contest is practical rather than merely formal. A contribution may need to become available, enter, remain, be encountered, be represented faithfully, and receive consideration. Control can therefore operate before present speech or debate, including through the sources from which later contest proceeds. Support: `debaets2026`, `tiffert2019`.
- The paper can also address unrealised expression or absent records where an established practice, process, or duty makes clear what would otherwise have been available. A merely imaginable absence is not enough.
- What would otherwise remain open to contest must be identifiable independently of the disputed interference. Otherwise the claim that contest has been short-circuited is circular.

## 2.2 When participation shortcuts contest

- Every real practice structures participation through remit, format, evidence, conduct, selection, ranking, jurisdiction, scarcity, and burdens of compliance. Restriction, burden, exclusion, unequal treatment, or outcome dependence therefore cannot by themselves distinguish censorship from ordinary organisation.
- The central distinction is functional. A rule, decision, or practice may apply the grounds relevant to the matter being contested, even when it excludes or defeats a contribution. It shortcuts contest when control of participation instead prevents those grounds from operating on the contribution, or allows them to operate only on a substituted or materially altered version of it. Support: `casadevallfang2009`, `dormandygrimley2024`, `hauswald2026`.
- The same intervention can therefore be ordinary with respect to one question and shortcut another. A journal-scope rule may decide fit to that journal while leaving scientific credibility open; control of the relevant venues could instead prevent claims from reaching serious scientific assessment. Jurisdiction or relocation is not intrinsically censorial or non-censorial. Support/background: `pralle2003`, `gieryn1983`.
- The interference must bear on the contribution because of its relation to what is at issue, whether through the speaker, source, topic, position, or channel. Pairwise differential treatment and censorial intent are not required: the pattern can be explicit or emergent, individual or distributed, active or produced through selective non-action, successful or failed.
- Several ordinary conditions can combine to create a shortcut even where no single condition does so alone. Accumulation by itself is not enough; their combined operation must take over something that would otherwise remain to contest.
- Evidence must support the alleged bypass rather than merely an unwanted outcome. Labels such as scope, relevance, quality, safety, conduct, or compliance do not settle what an operation is doing, while correlation or aggregate disparity alone does not establish censorship. Process evidence, reasons actually given, temporal changes, comparators, appeal routes, and alternative forums can help; some cases will remain empirically underdetermined.

# 3. Familiar Forms and Effects

- Familiar censorship vocabulary remains useful descriptively. **Exclusion** blocks or removes a contribution; **attenuation** leaves it present but harder to encounter or sustain; **transformation** alters the version through which it participates; **saturation** changes the surrounding conditions through burden or displacement. These terms describe what can happen without independently classifying a case as censorship. Support/background: `debaets2026`, `tiffert2019`, `roberts2018`, `gillespie2022`, `howardkira2026`, `zheng2024`, `kingpanroberts2017`.
- **Self-censorship** and chilling are responses to surrounding conditions, not separate criteria for censorship. Expected criticism, embarrassment, disagreement, or rebuttal can rationally discourage speech while remaining part of ordinary contest. Where the surrounding conditions themselves shortcut contest, people may respond by withholding expression or by altering what they say; the effect need not be silence. Support: `penney2022`, `dotson2011`, `weeks2024`, `yuenlee2025`, `kuran1995`, `wangmayer2023`.
- Censorship can persist through rules, classifications, rankings, routines, infrastructure, expectations, or norms. Persistence, institutionalisation, or historical origin does not create a separate type. Support/background: `yuenlee2025`, `foucault1977`, `bowkerstar1999`, `star1999`.

# 4. Cases and Conceptual Contrasts

- The cases form a developmental sequence rather than a checklist. They move from a direct departure from an identifiable contest toward increasingly indirect alterations of the conditions under which contest occurs.
- The sequence successively removes assumptions that censorship requires exclusion, alteration of the contribution itself, or speech already present in current contest. It then asks whether mechanisation changes the underlying relation or mainly its scale and implementation.
- The cases are deliberately asymmetric. Translation, Tiffert's source-base redaction, and explicit political search filtering are the strongest worked positive applications. Withdrawal isolates bypass of scholarly assessment; saturation develops burden and displacement; self-censorship and chilling, popularity feedback, personalised curation, jurisdiction, moderation, and constitutive norms test boundaries or evidential insufficiency.

## 4.1 Contest and circumvention — peer review and withdrawal

- Scientific evaluation gives a clear first contrast because exclusion can occur both through scientific assessment and around it. The Schön retractions followed investigation into measurement validity; measurement validity is itself a scientific ground bearing on the claims, so the papers' standing changed through scientific evaluation rather than by bypassing it. Support: `casadevallfang2009`, `beasley2002`, `schoen2003retraction`.
- Peer-review and gatekeeping literature makes the boundary explicit. Rejecting a paper because relevant scientific reasons have been applied against it can be part of scientific contest even though the result is exclusion; journal scope can allocate venue without deciding broader scientific credibility. Gatekeeping becomes structurally different where it prevents theories, objections, or evidence from receiving serious consideration for reasons that control participation rather than engage their scientific merits. Support: `atkinson1994`, `casadevallfang2009`, `dormandygrimley2024`, `hauswald2026`.
- Gilley's "The Case for Colonialism" passed double-blind peer review and was later withdrawn after serious or credible threats to the editor. Whatever the article's scholarly merits, the threat did not engage them: it determined continued availability after that evaluation. The case therefore isolates circumvention without implying that peer review itself is non-selective or non-gatekeeping. Support: `gilley2017`, `taylorfrancis2017withdrawal`.

## 4.2 Presence without faithful participation — translation

- Translation shows why censorship need not require exclusion: a source may remain available while participating through an altered representation.
- Translation Studies has confronted the demarcation problem directly. The 2012 Translation Studies Forum debates whether broad, constitutive conceptions of censorship risk collapsing translation's unavoidable selection and transformation into censorship itself. Karoubi likewise argues that constitutive theories can make exclusion and control indiscriminately censorial and proposes a Bourdieusian field account to disambiguate censorship in translation. The present account shares that demarcational aim but locates the boundary differently: transformation as such is not censorship; the question is whether selective intervention changes what a source can contribute before readers can engage it. Support: `baermullerstpierreocuilleanain2012`, `karoubi2025`.
- Zheng reports ordinary corrections alongside directional political revisions in Chinese translations concerning EU--Russia relations: criticism of Russia was generally softened while negative evaluation of the EU was strengthened. The asymmetry matters because it distinguishes the case from transformation merely as an unavoidable feature of translation. Support: `zheng2024`.
- The translated source is presented as bearing on readers' judgments of legitimacy and blame concerning Russia and the EU. It remains present, but politically selective revision changes what it can contribute before readers encounter and assess its presented position. The claim is local to that represented contribution; it does not require showing that the translations determined the wider downstream standing of Russia or the EU.

## 4.3 Burden and displacement — saturation

- Saturation matters because a shortcut can act on the surroundings of contest without removing or modifying the contribution itself. One route is burden: filtering, checking, answering, or recovering the matter at issue becomes costly without resolving it. Another is displacement: attention and response are redirected onto another matter. Legal discovery offers a useful burden analogy: scholarship describes burdensome requests or productions as strategic means of imposing costs, including productions in which responsive material is submerged in irrelevant documents. Support/background: `sharpe2009`, `arbor2016`.
- Counter-speech is the limiting contrast. Even overwhelming rebuttal can remain contest when the difficulty comes from substantive engagement with the matter at issue rather than from surrounding conditions that substitute for engagement.
- King, Pan, and Roberts estimate roughly 448 million government-fabricated social-media comments per year, find that the operation largely avoids arguing with critics, and infer a strategy of distraction around collective-action discussions and events. This is strong evidence of a strategy fitted to displacement, but not by itself proof that every deployment successfully displaced practical attention. Support: `kingpanroberts2017`; background: `roberts2018`.

## 4.4 Before speech or evidence reaches contest — self-censorship and sources

- Anticipated criticism, embarrassment, or rebuttal can rationally discourage or alter expression while remaining part of ordinary contest. Self-censorship becomes relevant to this account where the surrounding conditions themselves shortcut contest, inducing people to withhold or reshape a contribution before the matter can be contested. Dotson's testimonial smothering and Penney's account of chilling as conformity show why the response may be altered speech rather than silence. Support/background: `dotson2011`, `penney2022`, `kuran1995`.
- Wang and Mayer report increased deletion or account restriction and reduced politically sensitive posting around Hong Kong's National Security Law. The evidence supports a population-level response to the legal environment without establishing every individual's motive or making every decision not to post a separate instance of censorship. Support: `wangmayer2023`; background: `weeks2024`, `yuenlee2025`, `kuran1995`.
- Control before contest need not act on a speaker. Tiffert documents stealth redaction of Chinese digital historical databases and reverse-engineers a politically sanitising pattern of removals. Records that would bear on historical claims are selectively removed before scholars can contest their credibility or significance. This is the stronger source-base case because the evidence reaches the intervention rather than merely a downstream absence. Support: `tiffert2019`, `debaets2026`.

## 4.5 Same shortcut under mechanisation — ranking and recommendation

- Computational mediation can change scale, speed, persistence, opacity, personalisation, reach, and interventionist capacity without requiring a separate criterion for censorship. A rank position is not automatically the larger matter at issue; the question is whether software controls what can participate in broader contests over visibility, salience, credibility, authority, or other forms of standing. Support/background: `cobbe2021`, `elkinkoren2020`.
- **Explicit political filtering** is the strongest positive mechanised case. Knockel, Kato, and Dirks identify more than 60,000 censorship rules across eight China-accessible search platforms, including hard censorship returning no results and soft censorship restricting results to selected or authorised sources. Query-sensitive political and religious rules determine source eligibility before retrieval and ranking, so disallowed sources cannot bear on public encounter with the issue. Support: `knockelkatodirks2023`.
- **Ambient feedback** shows why algorithmic inequality is not enough. Cho and Roy show that popularity-first ranking can reinforce existing popularity and delay discovery of new pages; Introna and Nissenbaum identify systematic prominence and exclusion that can arise by design or accidentally. Existing prominence can therefore feed back into future visibility without a censorial intention, but unequal attention is not censorship merely because it is unequal. Support: `choroy2004`, `intronanissenbaum2000`.
- **Personalised curation** supplies a second boundary. González-Bailón et al. show that Facebook's computational curation materially changed the ideological composition of political news actually encountered relative to what was potentially available. This establishes structuring of the informational arena, not by itself censorship, downstream belief change, or a bypass of a particular contest. Support: `gonzalezbailon2023`.
- Xin, Zheng, and Ni's `discursive preclusion` sharpens the evidential limit: strong political/non-political and cross-platform differences in visible discourse can reveal infrastructural structuring while still leaving proprietary filtering mechanisms underdetermined. Mechanisation can therefore make control of participation powerful without making every mediated outcome censorship. Support: `xinzhengni2026`.

## 4.6 Boundary contrasts

- **Jurisdiction and venue:** arena choice can change strategic prospects without shortcutting contest, while boundaries can also be actively drawn to allocate membership, authority, or resources. Relocation therefore establishes neither censorship nor its absence; the issue is whether control of the forum itself takes over something that would otherwise remain to substantive contest. Support: `pralle2003`; background: `gieryn1983`.
- **Moderation:** one intervention can legitimately decide one question while shortcutting another. A conduct rule may settle what behaviour a forum permits while also controlling whether a position can participate in a separate contest over public credibility or legitimacy. The fact that Reddit's 2015 bans reduced hate speech therefore does not by itself settle what relation is being analysed or whether it is censorial. Support: `chandrasekharan2017`; background: `gillespie2022`, `howardkira2026`.
- **Settled norms and constitutive structure:** stigma, convention, coordination, civility, professional norms, and constitutive constraints can shape participation without being censorship. Ambient pressure is evidence to analyse, not a censorship label. Where we cannot identify what has been bypassed and how the surrounding conditions did that work, the effect remains outside the present classification even if it is socially important. Support: `beagan2022`, `holman2022`; background: `bourdieu1991`, `butler1998`, `moore2013`, `foucault1977`, `bowkerstar1999`, `star1999`.

# 5. Limits

- The account is not a universal decision procedure or quantitative censoriality score. Counterfactuals, comparators, process traces, and other probes can aid explanation without becoming necessary or sufficient tests.
- Hard cases are expected where the practices setting the terms of participation are entangled with the contest itself, or where available evidence establishes an outcome without establishing how it was produced. The account should locate that uncertainty rather than force a binary classification.
- Interacting conditions may produce censorship at system level even where no component is independently censorial, but accumulation alone is insufficient.
- The level at which a shortcut occurs need not match the level at which downstream discourse is measured. Compensation, resistance, amplification, or backfire can coexist with locally reduced participation. Support/background: `simpson2024`.
- Structural description does not determine lawfulness, ethics, proportionality, necessity, professional appropriateness, or legitimacy.

---

# 6. Conclusion

- Control of participation can explain common structure across overt, indirect, distributed, representational, self-censoring, upstream, and mechanised forms of censorship without making all constrained communication censorial.
- The explanatory gain comes from separating what happens within contest from interventions that take over something contest would otherwise have to do. Exclusion, transformation, saturation, self-censorship, source control, and computational filtering can all matter without becoming separate theories of censorship.
- The strongest result is explanatory compression. Difficult cases become comparable without being made identical, and uncertainty can remain visible where evidence does not support classification. The theory should therefore be judged by whether it clarifies more with less machinery, not by whether it closes every possible objection.
