# Toward a Structural Account of Censorship

## Global constraints

- Scope: censorship, not a general theory of morality, social control, political legitimacy, propaganda, persuasion, or social shortcuts.
- Descriptive first: identify censorial structure before evaluating justification, benefit, harm, proportionality, or legality.
- Cases are conceptual contrasts and boundary cases; sensitive cases are used because difficult boundaries are unusually visible, not as political advocacy or a historical survey.
- Theory discipline: retain only concepts that clarify or compress the shortcut account.

### Source discipline

- Literature, empirical, historical, and attributed claims require verified support; hypothetical structural comparisons do not.
- Structural censorship and the idea that censorship can foreclose, frustrate, or short-circuit inquiry have clear antecedents; novelty is not claimed for those ideas by themselves.
- The authored contribution is the relational account developed here and the explanatory compression it produces; analytic questions and descriptive vocabulary are tools, not separate novelty claims.

# 1. Positioning and Contribution

## Problem and lineage

- Censorship scholarship already extends beyond overt prohibition to structural/constitutive, self-censoring, frictional, and flooding forms; breadth beyond bans is not the novelty claim. Support: `jansen1988`, `bourdieu1991`, `butler1998`, `muller2004`, `freshwater2004`, `bunn2015`, `roberts2018`, `debaets2026`.
- Bourdieu is a direct antecedent to the field-based view: field structure can govern both access to expression and expressive form through its sanctions. Moore identifies the corresponding danger that structural accounts become so expansive that conditions of speech and censorship cease to be distinguishable. Support: `bourdieu1991`, pp. 137--159; `moore2013`.
- This is the demarcation problem: mere restriction or structuring of communicative possibility cannot by itself distinguish censorship from communication, selection, or social organisation. Support: `schauer1998`, `butler1998`, `muller2004`, `freshwater2004`, `bunn2015`, `debaets2026`.
- The present account therefore does not assume that every constitutive constraint is censorial. Where an independently identifiable target, ordering, and selective shortcut cannot be recovered, a condition may structure speech, intelligibility, or participation without being classified as censorship here. This is a deliberate boundary against the most expansive constitutive uses rather than a denial that such structuring matters. Support: `bourdieu1991`, `butler1998`, `moore2013`.
- Steward supplies a close antecedent to the core intuition: censorship can foreclose or frustrate inquiry, so apparent consensus can reflect a short-circuit of inquiry rather than successful contest. Support: `steward2014`, especially introduction pp. 2--4.
- Atkinson supplies a direct peer-review analogue, arguing that editorial selection can pre-empt scientific debate and determine the exposure of new ideas. Support: `atkinson1994`.
- Bachrach and Baratz supply a broader power-theoretic antecedent: nondecision-making can operate by limiting what reaches decision through dominant values, institutions, and procedures. This is not itself a censorship theory, but it clarifies how practical outcomes can be produced upstream of overt contest. Support: `bachrachbaratz1962`, `bachrachbaratz1963`.
- This account generalises **inquiry** to **contest**: field-specific processes through which practical ordering can be established, defended, or changed, including representation, visibility, artistic expression, self-presentation, attention, and standing as well as argument over claims.
- Cobbe argues that algorithmic censorship can be distinctive in reach, interventionist capacity, private ordering, and structural power. The present claim is narrower and demarcational: those implementation-level differences can matter greatly without by themselves supplying a different criterion for when a relation is censorial. Support: `cobbe2021`.
- Elkin-Koren likewise treats AI content filters as carrying censorial power capable of bypassing traditional checks and balances and proposes an adversarial `Contesting Algorithms` procedure to restore public oversight. This is a close procedural antecedent for contestation of algorithmic filtering; its object is accountable content-filtering governance rather than a general demarcational criterion for distinguishing censorial from non-censorial participation conditions. Support: `elkinkoren2020`.
- Xin, Zheng, and Ni introduce **discursive preclusion** for anticipatory infrastructural governance that conditions what can become visible or expressible before post-hoc moderation. This is a direct near-neighbour for the pre-entry and mechanised cases, but temporal position is not demarcational here: upstream filtration is censorial only where evidence identifies a selective shortcut around an independently identifiable contest. Their own Xiaohongshu account is explicitly diagnostic rather than demonstrative because the filtering mechanism is inferred from visible outcomes rather than directly observed. Support: `xinzhengni2026`.
- `Contestability` is not introduced as a new term. Democratic and contestatory theories use it for the effective openness of governments or public policies to challenge, including citizens' practical opportunity to participate in public contestation and the possibility that formally open systems can remain weakly contestable. Content-moderation research also applies contestability to platform decisions. Support: `girard2015`, `trantidis2017`, `vaccaro2021`.
- The present use is field-relative and target-relative rather than a criterion of democratic legitimacy: it specifies the target's practical opportunity to participate in the contest through which an identifiable ordering can change. The contribution lies in relating the structuring of that opportunity to censorship, not in the term `contestability` itself.
- Bonotti and Seglow develop a normative relational theory of free speech grounded in recognition and republican non-domination, including substantive opportunities to communicate and contest collective decisions; Bishop's forthcoming reconsideration uses censorship cases to press that relational framework beyond recognition alone. The present account addresses a different question: it is a descriptive demarcation account of when participation conditions are censorial, prior to whether the resulting relation is justified or valuable. Support: `bonottiseglow2022`, `bishopforthcoming`.
- Hornsby and Langton's illocutionary account makes a closely related participation point: locution can remain physically possible while communicative agency is disabled through failure of the uptake conditions required for some speech acts. Dotson likewise analyses testimonial silencing through failures of hearers to meet speaker dependency in linguistic exchange. These are direct antecedents for the claim that presence is not sufficient for effective participation, but they concern particular forms of communicative or epistemic agency rather than a general criterion of censorship. Support: `hornsbylangton1998`, `dotson2011`.
- Young distinguishes external exclusion from internal exclusion within nominally inclusive democratic processes. Anttila and Domínguez-Armas adapt that lineage into a general account of **argumentative exclusion**, distinguishing external/internal, active/passive, and complete/partial exclusion and allowing structural and algorithmic obstacles and failures of uptake. This is the nearest participation-side antecedent to the present account. The difference is demarcational and in scope: the target here need not be an arguer or even realised speech; the relevant contest need not be propositional argument; and denial, weakness, or failure of participation is not by itself sufficient for censorship. A censorial classification additionally requires a selective participation-layer operation that substitutes for first-order work on an independently identifiable practical ordering. Support: `young2000`, `anttiladominguezarmas2025`.
- Aikin and Casey's account of the **free speech fallacy** supplies a complementary negative constraint: restriction or exclusion cannot itself be used as meta-evidence that critical exchange has been corrupted or that the excluded position is epistemically strengthened. The present account likewise treats exclusion, criticism, defeat, and procedural restriction as insufficient; its positive classification concerns the independently evidenced relation between participation control and first-order contest, not vindication of the target. Support: `aikincasey2023`.
- Bejan's relational ideal of **equal speech** similarly distinguishes mere freedom to express from a claim to address others and receive hearing and consideration, with speakers treated on a par. The present use of contestability is descriptive and intentionally weaker: it does not posit a right to equal hearing, consideration, uptake, influence, or parity. Being ignored, rejected, defeated, or outnumbered within contest is not censorial merely because participation is ineffective; the shortcut relation must still be shown. Support: `bejan2020`.

## Proposed contribution

- Core proposal: **a censorial relation arises where participation conditions selectively structure a target's contestability so that a participation-layer determination substitutes for, prejudges, or forecloses first-order work on a practical ordering that would otherwise remain open to the processes by which standing on that ordering is established, defended, or changed**.
- The contribution is explanatory and demarcational: show a common relation across overt, indirect, distributed, representational, and anticipatory phenomena while preserving meaningful differences from ordinary criticism, selection, ranking, editing, moderation, organisation, and error.

# 2. Contestability and the Shortcut

## 2.1 Ordering, contest, target, and contestability

- **Ordering**: the practical distribution of field-specific standing such as credibility, legitimacy, admissibility, visibility, salience, recognition, or authority.
- **Contest**: the field-specific process through which that ordering is established, defended, or changed through expression, representation, claims, reasons, evidence, interpretation, criticism, rebuttal, comparison, or challenges to standing or admissibility. It includes art and self-presentation without requiring propositional argument.
- **First-order contest**: for a specified ordering, the part of field practice that applies the reasons, evidence, interpretations, comparisons, responses, or other evaluative relations whose application to a target directly constitutes or revises its standing on that ordering. This is functional rather than institutional: the same journal, platform, court, gallery, archive, audience, or other institution can both conduct first-order contest and control participation in it.
- **Target**: an expression, speaker, source, record, representation, position, topic, or channel whose actual or potential bearing on an ordering is structured. A target need not already have entered contest or have been realised as an expression or record.
- Potentiality is bounded by independently identifiable field practice: a merely imaginable expression or record is not a target. In pre-creation cases, the potential contribution must be recoverable from an established process, practice, or duty of creation or preservation rather than inferred solely from its absence.
- **Contestability**: practical opportunity for a target to become available to, enter, remain in, be encountered, be represented, and receive consideration within that contest.
- Contestability can therefore be structured upstream of present expression or debate. De Baets notes that records may never be created, may be destroyed, hidden, kept secret, or selectively accessible; Tiffert documents stealth redaction of Chinese digital historical databases. Such source-base control is relevant where it selectively does work on a later ordering before later participants can encounter the evidence. Support: `debaets2026`, `tiffert2019`.
- The field, ordering, and first-order processes by which standing on that ordering is established, defended, or changed must be identifiable independently of the disputed participation condition. Otherwise the claim that contest has been short-circuited is circular.
- `Ordinary` remains descriptive, not exculpatory, but ordinary practice does not itself settle the classification. A settled institutional practice can contain both first-order evaluation and participation-layer controls; the analysis must identify which function the disputed operation performs relative to the specified ordering.
- Fields necessarily structure participation through remit, format, evidence, conduct, selection, ranking, jurisdiction, scarcity, and burdens of compliance. Burdens can themselves be politically shaped, but restriction, burden, exclusion, or outcome dependence alone is insufficient. Background: `moynihanherdharvey2015`.
- A participation condition **organises** contest when it allocates venue, format, timing, scarcity, or eligibility while leaving the target's standing on the specified ordering to first-order contest. It **conducts** contest when its effect on participation is itself an application of the first-order grounds by which that standing is decided. It **shortcuts** contest when a participation-layer basis controls availability, representation, attention, routing, or eligibility so that those first-order grounds cannot operate on the target, or can operate only on a substituted or materially altered target. The distinction is therefore not whether a condition is selective or outcome-determining---ordinary gatekeeping can be both---but whether it applies the ordering's first-order evaluative grounds or controls access to their application. Support: `casadevallfang2009`, `dormandygrimley2024`, `hauswald2026`.
- The same intervention can therefore conduct one ordering while shortcutting another. A journal-scope decision may directly conduct the ordering of fit-to-journal while merely routing a paper elsewhere for the broader ordering of scientific credibility; if control of the relevant venues instead prevents the paper's scientific claims from reaching the processes by which that credibility is assessed, the same kind of gatekeeping can become part of a shortcut relative to that broader ordering. The ordering under analysis must always be named.
- Jurisdiction allocates contest among arenas, but relocation is not neutral by definition: venue choice can change strategic prospects, while boundaries can be actively drawn and redrawn to allocate membership, authority, or resources. Support/background: `pralle2003`, `gieryn1983`.
- No class of participation condition is intrinsically outside the analysis: formal, procedural, jurisdictional, evidentiary, classificatory, temporal, or other conditions can either organise contest or, in their operation, become part of a shortcut around it.

## 2.2 Censorial relations

- A positive classification requires the ordering and its first-order contest to be independently identifiable, the participation condition or configuration to structure a target's contestability selectively with respect to its bearing on that ordering, and that selective operation to bypass rather than apply the first-order grounds of the ordering by taking over work those grounds would otherwise leave open. These are mutually constraining relations in an explanation rather than independent boxes to tick, a score, or a universal decision procedure.
- The unit of analysis can therefore be a single participation condition or an interacting configuration of conditions. No individual component need independently perform the shortcut if their combined operation does; accumulation of ordinary burdens or disadvantages is not enough unless the configuration itself satisfies selectivity and shortcutting.
- Causal or outcome dependence is insufficient. Counterfactual comparison can sometimes clarify whether a condition leaves the standing at issue open to contest or takes over some of that work, but no single counterfactual is constitutive of a censorial relation.
- Dominance, popularity, resource advantage, majority support, or repeated success within contest are not censorial merely because they make minority participation ineffective. Shortcut analysis becomes relevant where the resulting standing is fed back into participation conditions so that prior dominance does work on the current ordering that would otherwise remain open to contest.
- Both the first-order evaluative function and the participation-layer function must be characterised independently of the disputed application. Institutional labels such as scope, relevance, quality, evidence, conduct, safety, or compliance do not self-certify which function is being performed; explicit rules, stated remit, established practice, process traces, reasons actually given, temporal changes, comparators, appeal or correction routes, and alternative forums can help distinguish application of first-order grounds from control over access to them.
- **Selectivity** asks how the shortcut tracks the target's bearing on the ordering through a target, topic, position, or channel. Pairwise differential treatment is not required. Where meaningful comparators exist, they may help reveal whether apparently ordinary grounds account for the condition's operation or whether its operation varies with the target's bearing on the ordering.
- Outcome disparity or correlation alone is insufficient. Where the organising function, jurisdiction, or relation to the ordering cannot be independently established, the case may remain empirically underdetermined rather than being resolved by definition.
- Classification is local to the relation rather than inferred from aggregate discourse. A censorial relation can fail, provoke compensating speech, or coexist with greater total attention; aggregate amplification does not undo a selective shortcut affecting the target. Simpson's account of a possible group-level `heating effect` despite individual self-censorship makes the level distinction explicit. Support/background: `simpson2024`.
- Intent, unified agency, deliberate action, and success are not defining: a relation can be intentional or emergent, individual or distributed, active or produced through selective non-action, successful or failed.

## 2.3 Questions for explanation

- What target, field, and practical ordering are involved, and which reasons, evidence, interpretations, comparisons, responses, or other relations directly constitute first-order work on that ordering?
- What role does the participation condition or configuration play relative to those first-order processes, including any allocation among forums, and how can both roles be characterised independently of the disputed application?
- How does the disputed operation structure the target's actual or potential participation: does it organise or route contest, apply the first-order grounds of the specified ordering, or control access to their application and thereby take over work that would otherwise remain open?
- How is the condition or configuration related to the target's bearing on the ordering through a target, topic, position, or channel, and what evidence supports that account?
- What form does the relation take, at what level are its practical consequences observed, and what remains uncertain or underdetermined?

# 3. Descriptive Coverage

- Familiar censorship vocabulary is retained as descriptive coverage, not as a new taxonomy or set of criteria. These labels describe forms a censorial relation can take; none classifies a case independently of the relational account above.
- **Exclusion** includes prevention and erasure: blocking or removing expression, access, entry, recognition, standing, or potential source material before later contest. Support/background: `debaets2026`, `tiffert2019`.
- **Attenuation** includes demotion, suppression, friction, delay, and containment: the target remains present but becomes harder to encounter, access, or sustain. Support: `roberts2018`, `gillespie2022`, `howardkira2026`.
- **Transformation** alters the version through which a target participates, including editing, translation, redaction, classification, framing, or substitution. Support: `zheng2024`.
- **Saturation** alters the surrounding field through noise, diversion, repetition, competing material, or volume. It can operate through burden, where surrounding conditions make participation costly without the matter being resolved through contest, or through displacement, where attention and response are redirected onto another matter. Substantive criticism and ordinary changes of agenda can remain part of contest. Support: `roberts2018`, `kingpanroberts2017`; legal analogy/background: `sharpe2009`, `arbor2016`.
- **Anticipatory restraint, anticipatory transformation, and self-censorship** are possible responses to ambient participation conditions, not separate censorial criteria. Chilling can alter what is expressed as well as whether expression occurs: Penney argues that such effects often produce more norm-conforming speech or conduct rather than silence, while Dotson's testimonial smothering identifies coerced truncation in anticipation of an audience unable or unwilling to provide appropriate uptake. Anticipatory transformation is therefore an intersection of pre-entry conditions with transformation, not a new censorial mechanism. Expected criticism or disagreement can remain ordinary contest; the explanatory object is the surrounding condition that structures the cost, form, or possibility of participation. Support: `penney2022`, `dotson2011`, `weeks2024`, `yuenlee2025`, `kuran1995`, `wangmayer2023`.
- Censorial relations can persist through rules, classifications, rankings, routines, infrastructure, expectations, or norms. Persistence does not create a new censorial type, and historical origin alone does not determine the status of a current relation. Support/background: `yuenlee2025`, `foucault1977`, `bowkerstar1999`, `star1999`.

# 4. Cases and Conceptual Contrasts

- The cases form a developmental sequence. They move from a relatively direct departure from an identifiable contest toward increasingly indirect alterations of the conditions under which contest occurs.
- The sequence successively removes assumptions that censorship requires exclusion, alteration of the target itself, or a target already participating in present contest. Saturation shows that the shortcut can act on the surrounding conditions of contest; pre-entry effects show that it can alter entry, expressive form, or the source base from which later contest proceeds. Section 4.5 then returns to several of these relations under computational implementation, where scale and automation can alter reach, intervention, and power without requiring a different demarcational criterion.
- The cases are deliberately asymmetric. Translation, Tiffert's source-base redaction, and explicit political search filtering are the main worked positive applications because their evidence can identify the ordering, selectivity, and shortcut relation at the level claimed. Withdrawal isolates bypass of a merits process; saturation develops burden and displacement pathways without forcing classification; anticipatory response, popularity feedback, personalised curation, commercial ranking, and Section 4.6 test boundaries or evidential insufficiency.

## 4.1 Contest and circumvention — peer review and withdrawal

- Scientific and scholarly evaluation provides a comparatively clear illustration because exclusion can occur both **within** first-order contest and **around** it. The Schön retractions followed investigation into reported measurement validity; measurement validity is itself a ground bearing on scientific standing, so the target's standing was changed through first-order scientific evaluation rather than by a shortcut. Support: `casadevallfang2009`, `beasley2002`, `schoen2003retraction`.
- Peer-review and gatekeeping literature makes the harder boundary explicit. Rejecting a paper because the relevant scientific reasons have been applied against it can conduct contest even though the result is exclusion; journal scope can allocate venue without deciding broader scientific credibility. By contrast, gatekeeping can distort consensus when it prevents theories, objections, or evidence from receiving serious consideration for reasons that control participation rather than apply the first-order grounds of the scientific ordering. Support: `atkinson1994`, `casadevallfang2009`, `dormandygrimley2024`, `hauswald2026`.
- Gilley's "The Case for Colonialism" passed double-blind peer review and was later withdrawn after serious or credible threats to the editor. Relative to scholarly merits, the threat is not a first-order scholarly ground: it is a participation-layer condition operating after that evaluation and determining continued availability. The case therefore isolates the shortcut structure without implying that peer review itself is non-selective or non-gatekeeping. Support: `gilley2017`, `taylorfrancis2017withdrawal`.
- Harder cases arise where methodology, professional standards, disciplinary boundaries, or the gatekeeping process are themselves disputed. The framework does not resolve those disputes by relabelling one side as the merits process; evidence must identify the ordering at issue and distinguish reasons that directly constitute standing on it from conditions controlling whether those reasons can be applied.

## 4.2 Presence without faithful participation — translation

- Translation shows why censorship need not require exclusion: a source may remain available while participating through an altered representation.
- Zheng reports ordinary corrections alongside directional political revisions in Chinese translations concerning EU--Russia relations: criticism of Russia was generally softened while negative evaluation of the EU was strengthened. The directional asymmetry is evidence that revision tracks the source's political bearing rather than generic correction alone. Support: `zheng2024`.
- The ordering is not defined by the edited text's own output: it is the target-language judgment of legitimacy and blame concerning Russia and the EU to which the translated source is presented as contributing. The positive relation is local to that represented contribution: the translated source is the target, editorial revision is the participation condition, selectivity is supplied by the directional political pattern, and the shortcut consists in altering what the source contributes to those judgments before readers can assess its presented position. Zheng does not establish the downstream standing of Russia or the EU across the wider discourse, and the classification does not require that stronger claim.

## 4.3 Burden and displacement — saturation

- Saturation is a descriptive surface form rather than a distinct shortcut mechanism. Its importance is that surrounding material can restructure the practical conditions of contest without removing or modifying the target.
- One route is **burden**: filtering, checking, answering, or recovering the matter at issue can become costly in ways that impair practical participation without resolving that matter through contest. Complexity, extensive relevant evidence, or numerous substantive objections can themselves be ordinary contest; the explanatory interest here is burden imposed by surrounding conditions that does work otherwise left to that contest. Legal discovery provides a close analogy without itself being classified here as censorship: scholarship describes burdensome discovery requests or responses as strategic means of imposing costs and inducing premature settlement, and courts have sanctioned productions in which responsive material was embedded in large quantities of irrelevant documents. Support/background: `sharpe2009`, `arbor2016`.
- A second route is **displacement**: competing material can redirect attention and response onto a different question, so the original matter loses practical salience without being resolved. Redirection can also be ordinary agenda change; the interest here is in surrounding conditions that selectively move the contest elsewhere while doing work on the original ordering.
- Counter-speech is the limiting contrast. Scale, repetition, coordination, or difficulty alone does not create the relation: even overwhelming rebuttal can remain contest when the burden comes from substantive engagement with the matter at issue rather than from conditions that substitute for that engagement.
- King, Pan, and Roberts estimate roughly 448 million government-fabricated social-media comments per year, find that the operation largely avoids arguing with critics, and infer a strategy of distraction around collective-action discussions and events. This provides evidence of a strategy structurally fitted to displacement, not by itself evidence that any particular deployment successfully displaced practical attention. A censorial classification therefore still depends on evidence that the surrounding intervention actually structured the target's practical contestability in the relevant field. Support: `kingpanroberts2017`; background: `roberts2018`.

## 4.4 Before contest begins — entry, form, and sources

- Anticipated criticism, embarrassment, or rebuttal can rationally discourage or alter expression while remaining part of ordinary contest. Dotson's testimonial smothering provides an important boundary precedent because anticipatory self-modification can also be coerced by expected failures of uptake. The structural question here is narrower: whether the ambient condition selectively controls participation so as to substitute for first-order work on the relevant ordering, rather than whether anticipatory self-modification occurs. Support/background: `dotson2011`.
- Penney's account of chilling effects as social conformity makes the transformation point explicit: the response to uncertain sanctions may be more norm-conforming speech or conduct rather than silence. The same ambient condition can therefore produce restraint, transformed participation, or both. Support: `penney2022`; background: `kuran1995`.
- Wang and Mayer analyse roughly seven million historical tweets around Hong Kong's National Security Law and report increased deletion or account restriction and reduced politically sensitive posting after the law. Support: `wangmayer2023`.
- In this case the practical ordering concerns the public availability and salience of politically sensitive positions. The law changes conditions of entry before posting, response, criticism, and disagreement can occur; the observed removal and reduced posting support a population-level anticipatory response to the legal condition without establishing every user's motive. The analysis does not require treating every deletion, restriction, or decision not to post as a separate individual censorial relation. Social and reputational pressure remains a harder middle case. Background: `weeks2024`, `yuenlee2025`, `kuran1995`.
- Pre-entry operation need not act on a speaker. Tiffert documents stealth redaction of Chinese digital historical databases and reverse-engineers the logic of a sample of politically sanitising removals; De Baets treats records that are never created, destroyed, hidden, kept secret, or selectively accessible as part of censorship's source-base problem. Support: `tiffert2019`, `debaets2026`.
- Tiffert supplies the stronger positive pre-entry case because the evidence reaches the intervention rather than only a downstream absence. The ordering is the historical standing of claims ordinarily worked out through source criticism and interpretation; the records are targets whose availability to that process is altered; the documented political pattern supplies selectivity; and redaction removes evidentiary contributions before scholars can contest their credibility or significance. The source base therefore does work on later historical judgment that would otherwise remain to scholarly contest. Support: `tiffert2019`.

## 4.5 Same relations under mechanisation — ranking and recommendation

- Computational mediation can introduce distinctive affordances and power effects without requiring a different demarcational criterion. Search engines, ranking systems, and recommender feeds implement participation conditions through software; mechanisation can change scale, speed, persistence, opacity, personalisation, reach, and interventionist capacity. Cobbe's account makes the latter differences explicit. Elkin-Koren's `Contesting Algorithms` instead uses adversarial contestation as a governance remedy within AI filtering; the present use of contest is analytical, asking whether filtering or another participation condition itself satisfies the general censorial relation. An individual rank position is an immediate system output rather than automatically the wider practical ordering; the analysis concerns how that output structures participation in independently identifiable orderings such as discoverability, salience, credibility, or authority. Support/background: `cobbe2021`, `elkinkoren2020`.
- Xin, Zheng, and Ni sharpen the boundary from the opposite direction. Their `discursive preclusion` describes upstream infrastructural filtration and reports strong political/non-political and cross-platform differences in visible discourse, but their causal account is explicitly diagnostic because proprietary ranking and filtering mechanisms are not directly observed. On the present account, this is evidence of infrastructural structuring but not by itself enough to establish which selective participation condition took over work from contest. Support: `xinzhengni2026`.
- **Ambient feedback:** Cho and Roy show that search engines returning already-popular pages first can reinforce existing popularity and delay discovery of new pages; Introna and Nissenbaum likewise identify systematic prominence and exclusion that can arise by design or accidentally. Popularity ranking is not censorial merely because it produces inequality. The structural importance is that an ambient ordering can become an input to future visibility, allowing existing standing to reproduce itself through a mechanised participation condition without censorial intent. Support: `choroy2004`, `intronanissenbaum2000`.
- **Explicit political filtering:** Knockel, Kato, and Dirks identify more than 60,000 censorship rules across eight China-accessible search platforms, distinguishing hard censorship that returns no results from soft censorship that restricts results to selected or authorised sources. Their tests show that triggering depends on query content, that political motivations account for much of the censored-name pattern they classify, and that authorised-domain lists are dominated by Chinese state-approved news sites. The broader ordering is public visibility and accessibility of politically or religiously sensitive material; the search result is a participation channel rather than the ordering itself. The rule system supplies selectivity because eligibility changes with politically or religiously sensitive query content. Hard filtering excludes the target outright; authorised-source filtering is the sharper shortcut because source eligibility is settled before retrieval and ranking, so disallowed sources cannot bear on public encounter with the issue. Support: `knockelkatodirks2023`.
- **Commercial criterion substitution:** the European Commission found that results from competing comparison-shopping services were subject to generic ranking and adjustment mechanisms capable of demotion while Google's own comparison-shopping results received more favourable positioning and display and were not subject to the same demotions; the Court of Justice upheld the infringement finding in 2024. The legal holding is about competition, not censorship, and is used here as a structural comparator: proprietary interest can alter the participation conditions of an ostensibly general ranking process. The censorship question in an analogous communicative case would still depend on whether that substituted condition selectively does work on an independently identifiable ordering. Support/background: `europeancommission2017googleshopping`, `cjeu2024googleshopping`.
- **Personalised curation:** González-Bailón et al. compare the political news available to 208 million US Facebook users during the 2020 election with what algorithmic curation actually exposed and what users engaged with; ideological segregation increased from potential to actual exposure and again to engagement. This establishes that computational curation can materially change the ideological composition of the informational field presented to users and increase segregation relative to potential exposure. It does not by itself establish censorial selectivity, intent, downstream belief change, or a progression toward extremism. Its significance here is structural: personalised ranking can alter the practical arena in which contest occurs without removing the underlying material. Support: `gonzalezbailon2023`.
- These contrasts expose rather than erase the demarcation problem. Of the mechanised examples here, explicit political filtering is the strong positive case. Discursive preclusion, popularity feedback, and personalised curation remain structural or evidential contrasts showing that upstream or ordinary mediation can structure contestability without yet establishing the selective shortcut; commercial preference remains a comparator showing that external objectives can be substituted into ranking. The computer makes the asymmetries scalable and inspectable, but the same explanatory questions still decide what follows.

## 4.6 Boundary contrasts

- These are negative and boundary cases rather than incomplete positive classifications: each identifies a plausible effect on participation but withholds the censorship conclusion where ordinary field criteria or competing explanations have not been separated from relation to the ordering.
- **Jurisdiction and venue:** arena choice can change strategic prospects without shortcutting contest. Venue shopping can be part of ordinary political contest, and boundaries can themselves be actively constructed; relocation therefore establishes neither censorship nor its absence. The explanatory issue is whether forum control still leaves substantive contest meaningfully available or instead changes who or what can participate, or what can be decided, so that the arena itself does work on the ordering. Support: `pralle2003`; background `gieryn1983`.
- **Library acquisition:** unequal holdings and acquisition hesitancy can reveal ambient pressure, but budget, remit, duplication, format, and collection criteria can also explain selection. Evidence about actual decision criteria and credible comparators is needed. Support: `garry2015`, `mooretudor2024`; background `asheim1953`, `kidd2009`.
- **Moderation:** one intervention can reduce contestability for some material while protecting participation for others, and it can conduct one ordering while shortcutting another. A conduct rule may directly constitute standing on the ordering of permissible forum behaviour while also controlling whether a position can participate in a distinct ordering of public credibility or legitimacy. Chandrasekharan et al. report large reductions in hate speech after Reddit's 2015 bans of `r/fatpeoplehate` and `r/CoonTown`; the ban itself does not settle which ordering or relation is being analysed, and therefore does not settle which relations are censorial. Support: `chandrasekharan2017`; background `gillespie2022`, `howardkira2026`.
- **Dominance and resources:** overwhelming advocacy or unequal capacity, including unequal ability to purchase communicative access, can be part of ordinary contest; resource inequality and the minority experience of being unheard do not themselves classify the relation. The harder boundary appears where established dominance is reproduced through participation conditions that govern whether competing targets can enter, remain in, or be encountered in contest over the same ordering.
- **Settled norms and constitutive structure:** identity concealment can vary with professional or workplace climate, but stigma, convention, coordination, civility, professional norms, and constitutive constraints can also structure participation without supplying a recoverable censorial relation. Ambient pressure is evidence to analyse, not a censorship label by itself. If the target, ordering, and selective shortcut cannot be independently identified, the constitutive effect remains outside the account rather than being classified by breadth or severity alone. Support: `beagan2022`, `holman2022`; background `bourdieu1991`, `butler1998`, `moore2013`, `foucault1977`, `bowkerstar1999`, `star1999`.

# 5. Limits

- The account is not a universal decision procedure or quantitative censoriality score; some cases will remain empirically underdetermined.
- Counterfactuals, comparators, and other probes are aids to explanation, not necessary or sufficient tests for censorship.
- Hard cases are expected where the process producing an ordering and the disputed participation condition are partly the same process; the account should expose that entanglement rather than force a classification. The same condition may conduct one specified ordering and shortcut another, so disagreement about the relevant ordering can be substantive rather than merely terminological.
- Interacting conditions may produce a censorial relation at system level even where no component is independently censorial, but accumulation alone is insufficient; the configuration must still satisfy the relational criteria.
- The level at which a censorial relation is identified need not match the level at which downstream discourse is measured. Compensation, resistance, or backfire can coexist with locally reduced contestability.
- Structural description does not determine lawfulness, ethics, proportionality, necessity, professional appropriateness, or legitimacy.
- Restriction, inequality, content sensitivity, institutional criteria, inherited norms, omission, technical failure, algorithmic mediation, ranking disparity, personalisation, or unequal outcomes are not sufficient by themselves.

---

# 6. Conclusion

- Selective structuring of contestability can explain common structure across overt, indirect, distributed, representational, anticipatory, upstream, and mechanised phenomena without making all constrained communication censorial.
- The target need not already be speaking or present in current contest, and the censorial relation can arise from an interacting configuration rather than a single isolable condition; neither extension changes the core shortcut criterion.
- Computational implementation does not require a separate demarcational criterion: software can reproduce, scale, or combine familiar participation conditions even where automation materially changes reach, interventionist capacity, opacity, personalisation, or institutional power.
- The strongest result is explanatory compression: difficult cases become comparable without being made identical, the gatekeeping boundary turns on whether participation control substitutes for first-order work rather than on exclusion alone, and uncertainty is located in the specified orderings, relations, institutional entanglements, levels of analysis, and evidence rather than hidden by a binary label.
