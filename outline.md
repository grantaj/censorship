# Censorship as Shortcut: A Relational Account of Contestability

## Global constraints

- Scope: censorship, not a general theory of morality, social control, political legitimacy, propaganda, persuasion, or social shortcuts.
- Descriptive first: identify censorial structure before evaluating justification, benefit, harm, proportionality, or legality.
- Cases are conceptual contrasts and boundary cases; sensitive cases are used because difficult boundaries are unusually visible, not as political advocacy or a historical survey.
- Theory discipline: retain only concepts that clarify or compress the shortcut account.

### Source discipline

- Literature, empirical, historical, and attributed claims require verified support; hypothetical structural comparisons do not.
- Structural censorship and the idea that censorship can foreclose, frustrate, or short-circuit inquiry have clear antecedents; novelty is not claimed for those ideas by themselves.
- The authored contribution is the relational account developed here and the explanatory compression it produces; descriptive vocabulary is supporting coverage, not a second theory.

---

# 1. Positioning and Contribution

## Problem and lineage

- Censorship scholarship already extends beyond overt prohibition to structural or constitutive, self-censoring, frictional, and flooding forms; breadth beyond bans is not the novelty claim. Bourdieu shows how field structure can govern access to expression and expressive form, while Moore identifies the danger that sufficiently expansive structural accounts make ordinary conditions of communication difficult to distinguish from censorship. This is the demarcation problem: restriction or structuring of communicative possibility cannot by itself classify a relation as censorial. Support: `jansen1988`, `bourdieu1991`, `butler1998`, `muller2004`, `freshwater2004`, `bunn2015`, `moore2013`, `roberts2018`, `debaets2026`.
- A second lineage supplies the shortcut intuition. Steward argues that censorship can foreclose or frustrate inquiry, so apparent consensus may reflect a short-circuit of inquiry rather than successful contest. Atkinson makes the same structure visible in scientific gatekeeping, and Bachrach and Baratz show more generally how outcomes can be produced upstream by limiting what reaches decision. The present account generalises **inquiry** to **contest**: field-specific processes through which practical standing is established, defended, or changed, including representation, visibility, artistic expression, self-presentation, attention, and standing as well as argument over claims. Support: `steward2014`, `atkinson1994`, `bachrachbaratz1962`, `bachrachbaratz1963`.
- A third lineage concerns participation. Democratic and content-moderation work uses `contestability` for practical openness to challenge; relational free-speech theory, illocutionary silencing, testimonial silencing, and argumentative exclusion all show that formal presence or permission to speak is not the same as effective participation. Anttila and Domínguez-Armas are the closest participation-side antecedent, but their object is argumentative exclusion; here the target need not be an arguer or even realised speech, and impaired participation is not sufficient without the shortcut relation. The present use of contestability is therefore narrower and descriptive: the target's practical opportunity to participate in the processes through which a specified ordering can change. Support: `girard2015`, `trantidis2017`, `vaccaro2021`, `bonottiseglow2022`, `bishopforthcoming`, `hornsbylangton1998`, `dotson2011`, `young2000`, `anttiladominguezarmas2025`, `aikincasey2023`, `bejan2020`.
- Algorithmic and infrastructural scholarship extends these issues upstream. Cobbe emphasises the reach, interventionist capacity, private ordering, and structural power of algorithmic censorship; Elkin-Koren develops contestation as a governance response to AI filtering; Xin, Zheng, and Ni describe `discursive preclusion`, in which infrastructure conditions what becomes visible or expressible before post-hoc moderation. Xin, Zheng, and Ni are a particularly close upstream neighbour, but temporal position is not demarcational here and their own causal account remains diagnostic where proprietary mechanisms are not directly observed. Implementation, timing, or opacity does not by itself establish the shortcut relation. Support: `cobbe2021`, `elkinkoren2020`, `xinzhengni2026`.
- The account therefore occupies a middle position between overt-act theories and accounts on which constitutive constraint readily becomes censorship. Structural conditions can matter without all being censorial. The question is whether an identifiable participation-layer operation selectively takes over work on an independently identifiable practical ordering that would otherwise remain open to contest. Support: `bourdieu1991`, `butler1998`, `moore2013`.

## Proposed contribution

- Core proposal: **a censorial relation arises where participation conditions selectively structure a target's contestability so that a participation-layer determination substitutes for, prejudges, or forecloses first-order work on a practical ordering that would otherwise remain open to contest**.
- The contribution is explanatory and demarcational: the same relation can illuminate overt, indirect, distributed, representational, anticipatory, and mechanised phenomena while preserving meaningful differences from ordinary criticism, selection, ranking, editing, moderation, organisation, and error.

# 2. Contestability and the Shortcut

## 2.1 Ordering, contest, target, and contestability

- **Ordering**: the practical distribution of field-specific standing such as credibility, legitimacy, admissibility, visibility, salience, recognition, or authority.
- **Contest**: the field-specific process through which that ordering is established, defended, or changed through reasons, evidence, interpretation, criticism, representation, comparison, response, or challenges to standing or admissibility. Contest need not be propositional argument; art, self-presentation, visibility, and representation can participate in it.
- **First-order contest**: the part of field practice that applies the grounds through which a target's standing on the specified ordering is constituted or revised. This is a functional distinction, not an institutional one: the same journal, platform, court, gallery, archive, audience, or other institution can both conduct first-order contest and control participation in it.
- **Target**: an expression, speaker, source, record, representation, position, topic, or channel whose actual or potential bearing on an ordering is structured. A potential target must be recoverable from an established practice or process rather than inferred solely from an imagined absence.
- **Contestability**: practical opportunity for a target to become available to, enter, remain in, be encountered, be represented, and receive consideration within that contest. Contestability can therefore be altered before present expression or debate, including through control of the source base from which later contest proceeds. Support: `debaets2026`, `tiffert2019`.
- The ordering and the first-order processes that bear on it must be identifiable independently of the disputed participation condition. Otherwise the claim that contest has been short-circuited is circular.

## 2.2 Organising, conducting, and shortcutting contest

- Fields necessarily structure participation through remit, format, evidence, conduct, selection, ranking, jurisdiction, scarcity, and burdens of compliance. Restriction, burden, exclusion, selectivity, or outcome dependence therefore cannot by themselves distinguish censorship from ordinary field organisation.
- A participation condition **organises** contest when it allocates venue, format, timing, scarcity, or eligibility while leaving the relevant standing to first-order contest. It **conducts** contest when its effect on participation is itself an application of the first-order grounds by which that standing is decided. It **shortcuts** contest when a participation-layer basis controls availability, representation, attention, routing, or eligibility so that first-order grounds cannot operate on the target, or can operate only on a substituted or materially altered target. Support: `casadevallfang2009`, `dormandygrimley2024`, `hauswald2026`.
- The same intervention can conduct one ordering while shortcutting another. A journal-scope rule may decide fit-to-journal while leaving broader scientific credibility open; control of the relevant venues could instead prevent claims from reaching the processes through which that broader credibility is assessed. The ordering under analysis therefore matters, but jurisdiction or relocation is not intrinsically censorial or non-censorial. Support/background: `pralle2003`, `gieryn1983`.
- **Selectivity** concerns the relation between the shortcut and the target's bearing on the ordering. It need not take the form of pairwise differential treatment, and intent is not required: a relation can be intentional or emergent, individual or distributed, active or produced through selective non-action, successful or failed.
- A censorial relation can also arise from an interacting configuration rather than a single isolable condition. Ordinary burdens do not become censorial merely by accumulating; the relevant question is whether their combined operation selectively takes over work that would otherwise remain to contest.
- Evidence must support the alleged relation rather than merely the unwanted outcome. Labels such as scope, relevance, quality, safety, conduct, or compliance do not settle what an operation is doing, while correlation or aggregate disparity alone does not establish a shortcut. Some cases will therefore remain empirically underdetermined.

# 3. Descriptive Coverage

- Familiar censorship vocabulary is retained as descriptive coverage rather than a new taxonomy. **Exclusion** blocks or removes a target; **attenuation** leaves it present but harder to encounter or sustain; **transformation** alters the version through which it participates; **saturation** changes the surrounding field through burden or displacement. These describe possible forms of a censorial relation but do not classify one independently of the shortcut account. Support/background: `debaets2026`, `tiffert2019`, `roberts2018`, `gillespie2022`, `howardkira2026`, `zheng2024`, `kingpanroberts2017`.
- **Anticipatory restraint, anticipatory transformation, and self-censorship** are responses to ambient participation conditions, not separate censorial criteria. Expected criticism or disagreement can remain ordinary contest; the explanatory object is the surrounding condition that structures the cost, form, or possibility of participation. Support: `penney2022`, `dotson2011`, `weeks2024`, `yuenlee2025`, `kuran1995`, `wangmayer2023`.
- Censorial relations can persist through rules, classifications, rankings, routines, infrastructure, expectations, or norms. Persistence, institutionalisation, or historical origin does not create a separate censorial type. Support/background: `yuenlee2025`, `foucault1977`, `bowkerstar1999`, `star1999`.

# 4. Cases and Conceptual Contrasts

- The cases form a developmental sequence rather than a checklist. They move from a direct departure from an identifiable contest toward increasingly indirect alterations of the conditions under which contest occurs.
- The sequence successively removes assumptions that censorship requires exclusion, alteration of the target itself, or a target already participating in present contest. It then asks whether mechanisation changes the underlying relation or mainly its scale and implementation.
- The cases are deliberately asymmetric. Translation, Tiffert's source-base redaction, and explicit political search filtering are the strongest worked positive applications. Withdrawal isolates bypass of a merits process; saturation develops burden and displacement; anticipatory response, popularity feedback, personalised curation, jurisdiction, moderation, and constitutive norms test boundaries or evidential insufficiency.

## 4.1 Contest and circumvention — peer review and withdrawal

- Scientific evaluation gives a clear first contrast because exclusion can occur both **within** first-order contest and **around** it. The Schön retractions followed investigation into measurement validity; measurement validity is itself a ground bearing on scientific standing, so the target's standing changed through first-order evaluation rather than a participation-layer shortcut. Support: `casadevallfang2009`, `beasley2002`, `schoen2003retraction`.
- Peer-review and gatekeeping literature makes the boundary explicit. Rejecting a paper because relevant scientific reasons have been applied against it can conduct contest even though the result is exclusion; journal scope can allocate venue without deciding broader scientific credibility. Gatekeeping becomes structurally different where it prevents theories, objections, or evidence from receiving serious consideration for reasons that control participation rather than apply the first-order grounds of scientific standing. Support: `atkinson1994`, `casadevallfang2009`, `dormandygrimley2024`, `hauswald2026`.
- Gilley's "The Case for Colonialism" passed double-blind peer review and was later withdrawn after serious or credible threats to the editor. Relative to scholarly merits, the threat is not a first-order scholarly ground: it determines continued availability after that evaluation. The case therefore isolates circumvention without implying that peer review itself is non-selective or non-gatekeeping. Support: `gilley2017`, `taylorfrancis2017withdrawal`.

## 4.2 Presence without faithful participation — translation

- Translation shows why censorship need not require exclusion: a source may remain available while participating through an altered representation.
- Zheng reports ordinary corrections alongside directional political revisions in Chinese translations concerning EU--Russia relations: criticism of Russia was generally softened while negative evaluation of the EU was strengthened. The asymmetry matters because it distinguishes the case from transformation merely as an unavoidable feature of translation. Support: `zheng2024`.
- The relevant ordering is the target-language judgment of legitimacy and blame to which the translated source is presented as contributing. The source remains present, but politically selective revision changes what it can contribute before readers encounter and assess its presented position. The claim is local to that represented contribution; it does not require showing that the translations determined the wider downstream standing of Russia or the EU.

## 4.3 Burden and displacement — saturation

- Saturation matters because a shortcut can act on the surrounding conditions of contest without removing or modifying the target. It can operate through **burden**, where filtering, checking, answering, or recovering the matter at issue becomes costly without resolving it, or through **displacement**, where attention and response are redirected onto another matter. Legal discovery offers a useful burden analogy: scholarship describes burdensome requests or productions as strategic means of imposing costs, including productions in which responsive material is submerged in irrelevant documents. Support/background: `sharpe2009`, `arbor2016`.
- Counter-speech is the limiting contrast. Even overwhelming rebuttal can remain contest when the difficulty comes from substantive engagement with the matter at issue rather than from surrounding conditions that substitute for engagement.
- King, Pan, and Roberts estimate roughly 448 million government-fabricated social-media comments per year, find that the operation largely avoids arguing with critics, and infer a strategy of distraction around collective-action discussions and events. This is strong evidence of a strategy fitted to displacement, but not by itself proof that every deployment successfully displaced practical attention. Support: `kingpanroberts2017`; background: `roberts2018`.

## 4.4 Before contest begins — entry, form, and sources

- Anticipated criticism, embarrassment, or rebuttal can rationally discourage or alter expression while remaining part of ordinary contest. The relevant structural question is not whether self-censorship occurs but whether an ambient participation condition selectively makes entry or expression do work that would otherwise remain to contest. Dotson's testimonial smothering and Penney's account of chilling as conformity show why the response may be transformed speech rather than silence. Support/background: `dotson2011`, `penney2022`, `kuran1995`.
- Wang and Mayer report increased deletion or account restriction and reduced politically sensitive posting around Hong Kong's National Security Law. The evidence supports a population-level anticipatory response to the legal environment without establishing every individual's motive or making every decision not to post a separate censorial relation. Support: `wangmayer2023`; background: `weeks2024`, `yuenlee2025`, `kuran1995`.
- Pre-entry operation need not act on a speaker. Tiffert documents stealth redaction of Chinese digital historical databases and reverse-engineers a politically sanitising pattern of removals. Here the intervention reaches the source base itself: records that would bear on historical claims are selectively removed before scholars can contest their credibility or significance. This is the stronger positive pre-entry case because the evidence reaches the participation-layer intervention rather than merely a downstream absence. Support: `tiffert2019`, `debaets2026`.

## 4.5 Same relations under mechanisation — ranking and recommendation

- Computational mediation can change scale, speed, persistence, opacity, personalisation, reach, and interventionist capacity without requiring a separate criterion for censorship. A rank position is an immediate system output rather than automatically the wider practical ordering; the question is how software structures participation in independently identifiable orderings such as discoverability, salience, credibility, or authority. Support/background: `cobbe2021`, `elkinkoren2020`.
- **Explicit political filtering** is the strongest positive mechanised case. Knockel, Kato, and Dirks identify more than 60,000 censorship rules across eight China-accessible search platforms, including hard censorship returning no results and soft censorship restricting results to selected or authorised sources. Query-sensitive political and religious rules determine source eligibility before retrieval and ranking, so disallowed sources cannot bear on public encounter with the issue. Support: `knockelkatodirks2023`.
- **Ambient feedback** shows why algorithmic inequality is not enough. Cho and Roy show that popularity-first ranking can reinforce existing popularity and delay discovery of new pages; Introna and Nissenbaum identify systematic prominence and exclusion that can arise by design or accidentally. Existing standing can therefore become an input to future visibility without a censorial intention, but the feedback is not censorial merely because it produces unequal attention. Support: `choroy2004`, `intronanissenbaum2000`.
- **Personalised curation** supplies a second boundary. González-Bailón et al. show that Facebook's computational curation materially changed the ideological composition of political news actually encountered relative to what was potentially available. This establishes structuring of the practical informational arena, not by itself censorial selectivity, downstream belief change, or a shortcut around a specified ordering. Support: `gonzalezbailon2023`.
- Xin, Zheng, and Ni's `discursive preclusion` sharpens the evidential limit: strong political/non-political and cross-platform differences in visible discourse can reveal infrastructural structuring while still leaving proprietary filtering mechanisms underdetermined. Mechanisation can therefore make participation conditions powerful without making every mediated outcome classifiable as censorship. Support: `xinzhengni2026`.

## 4.6 Boundary contrasts

- **Jurisdiction and venue:** arena choice can change strategic prospects without shortcutting contest, while boundaries can also be actively drawn to allocate membership, authority, or resources. Relocation therefore establishes neither censorship nor its absence; the issue is whether forum control itself takes over work on the ordering. Support: `pralle2003`; background: `gieryn1983`.
- **Moderation:** one intervention can conduct one ordering while shortcutting another. A conduct rule may directly constitute standing on permissible forum behaviour while also controlling whether a position can participate in a distinct ordering of public credibility or legitimacy. The fact that Reddit's 2015 bans reduced hate speech therefore does not by itself settle which relation is being analysed or whether it is censorial. Support: `chandrasekharan2017`; background: `gillespie2022`, `howardkira2026`.
- **Settled norms and constitutive structure:** stigma, convention, coordination, civility, professional norms, and constitutive constraints can shape participation without supplying a recoverable censorial relation. Ambient pressure is evidence to analyse, not a censorship label. Where the target, ordering, and selective shortcut cannot be independently identified, the effect remains outside the present classification even if it is socially important. Support: `beagan2022`, `holman2022`; background: `bourdieu1991`, `butler1998`, `moore2013`, `foucault1977`, `bowkerstar1999`, `star1999`.

# 5. Limits

- The account is not a universal decision procedure or quantitative censoriality score. Counterfactuals, comparators, process traces, and other probes can aid explanation without becoming necessary or sufficient tests.
- Hard cases are expected where the process producing an ordering and the disputed participation condition are entangled, or where available evidence establishes an outcome without establishing the operation that produced it. The framework should locate that uncertainty rather than force a binary classification.
- Interacting conditions may produce a censorial relation at system level even where no component is independently censorial, but accumulation alone is insufficient.
- The level at which a censorial relation is identified need not match the level at which downstream discourse is measured. Compensation, resistance, amplification, or backfire can coexist with locally reduced contestability. Support/background: `simpson2024`.
- Structural description does not determine lawfulness, ethics, proportionality, necessity, professional appropriateness, or legitimacy.

---

# 6. Conclusion

- Selective structuring of contestability can explain common structure across overt, indirect, distributed, representational, anticipatory, upstream, and mechanised phenomena without making all constrained communication censorial.
- The explanatory gain comes from separating what happens **within** contest from participation-layer operations that take over work contest would otherwise have to do. That distinction survives changes in mechanism: exclusion, transformation, saturation, anticipatory response, and computational filtering can all matter without becoming separate theories of censorship.
- The strongest result is explanatory compression. Difficult cases become comparable without being made identical, positive and negative cases can differ in the role they play, and uncertainty can remain visible where evidence does not support classification. The theory should therefore be judged by whether it clarifies more with less machinery, not by whether it closes every possible objection.
