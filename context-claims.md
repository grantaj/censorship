# Key Claims Index
## Canonical Claims for Essay Coherence

This document lists the core claims the essay must maintain and support. Each section of the outline should connect to one or more of these claims. If a section cannot be mapped, the outline or claims list should be revised.

1. **Censorship is a shortcut mechanism that enforces epistemic preference** under conditions of complexity, disagreement, or power asymmetry.
2. **Censorship is not fundamentally about speech**; speech and media are surfaces for stabilising what counts as credible, legitimate, or real.
3. **Morality and censorship are structurally homologous shortcuts**, differing by mechanism (attraction vs constraint) rather than function.
4. **Conduct is the regulated variable** within an epistemic system, shaped jointly by moral attraction and censorial constraint.
5. **Censorship operates through composable mechanisms** (erasure, prevention, suppression, modification, substitution, delay, saturation, contradiction, delegitimation, inversion, containment, internalisation).
6. **Operational kinds must be kept separate from analytic axes** (scale, power distribution, participation, temporal stability, visibility, justification regime, domain of control).
7. **Legitimacy is continuous, not binary**, and tracks participation and revisability rather than mere stability or enforcement.
8. **Conflict arises from collisions between incompatible shortcut systems** and from exclusion in the formation or revision of epistemic preferences.
9. **Censorship is infrastructural and often invisible**, persisting even in open societies through implicit norms, incentives, and technical systems.
10. **The framework is descriptive, not justificatory**; moral or legal evaluation is deferred until after structural analysis.
