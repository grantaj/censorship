# Key Claims Index
## Canonical Claims for the Narrow Censorship Essay

This file lists the claims the essay must establish. `outline.md` is authoritative for argument order and detail; this index exists as a compact coherence check.

1. **Censorship is selective intervention in an epistemic environment that advances or stabilises an epistemic preference.** The preference concerns the ordering of claims, topics, representations, or speakers; the intervention operates through practical control surfaces such as availability, access, circulation, timing, visibility, ranking, legibility, standing, or memory.

2. **Censorship can function as an epistemic shortcut.** By changing the practical conditions of epistemic participation, censorial control can stabilise a preferred ordering without requiring that ordering to be continually re-established through epistemic contest. This is a structural claim about censorship, not a general theory of why social systems produce shortcuts.

3. **The boundary between epistemic contest and epistemic control is contextual rather than categorical.** Selection, curation, editing, moderation, ranking, peer review, and library acquisition can be ordinary epistemic practice, censorial control, or a mixture depending on how they function in context.

4. **Boundary analysis should expose rather than hide ambiguity.** Differential treatment, control of consequential channels or resources, mode of disadvantage, relation to epistemic preference, scarcity, institutional remit, substitutability, pattern, opacity, and power all bear on censorial character; no actor label mechanically settles the classification.

5. **Censorship operates through a compact set of operational families at a common level of abstraction:** exclusion, attenuation, transformation, saturation/flooding, and induced self-censorship. Useful subtypes and strategies should not be promoted to peer categories merely because they have distinct names.

6. **Delegitimation, inversion, contradiction, and selection are boundary-sensitive strategies or practices rather than primary operational families.** Their censorial significance depends on whether they produce exclusion, attenuation, saturation, transformation, or induced self-censorship.

7. **Operational families must be kept separate from descriptive dimensions.** Relevant dimensions include scale, power and channel control, participation in preference formation, persistence/revisability, visibility/opacity, justification regime, and control surface/domain. These dimensions are analytically separable, not claimed to be mathematically orthogonal or empirically independent.

8. **Censorial shortcuts can sediment past contest into present infrastructure.** A rule, classification, ranking, norm, archive practice, or other settlement can preserve the outcome of an earlier epistemic struggle so that later participants encounter the settled participation conditions without necessarily encountering the argument or conflict that produced them. Revisability describes, in part, whether that inherited settlement can practically be reopened to contest.

9. **Routinisation can reduce the burden of overt enforcement without proving acceptance or legitimacy.** As censorial conditions are reproduced through infrastructure, professional routine, expectations, or anticipatory restraint, maintaining the preferred ordering may require fewer visible interventions. Reduced enforcement is evidence about mechanism and persistence, not about consent, truth, or justification.

10. **The visibility and experience of censorial control can be position-dependent without making classification subjective.** A settled arrangement may appear as ordinary quality control, safety, professionalism, or common sense to those advantaged by its epistemic ordering while being salient as exclusion or censorship to those disadvantaged by it. This asymmetry is a reason to analyse structure rather than rely on institutional labels or participants' self-descriptions.

11. **Saturation can suppress epistemic discrimination itself, not only bury a particular target.** Flooding, diversion, repetition, or environmental noise can make distinguishing, verifying, or acting on claims sufficiently costly or uncertain that participants disengage or treat resolution as futile. The censorial effect need not install a positive counter-belief; it can prevent a rival claim from acquiring enough standing to matter.

12. **Censorship can be infrastructural, partial, probabilistic, and low-visibility.** Friction, ranking, classification, routines, access rules, flooding, and anticipatory restraint can matter even when expression remains technically possible; infrastructure can turn episodic interventions into standing shortcuts around repeated contest.

13. **Classification and evaluation are separate tasks.** Calling an intervention censorship does not determine whether it is lawful, justified, proportionate, harmful, necessary, democratic, professionally appropriate, or effective; calling it moderation, curation, safety, or quality control does not determine that it is not censorship.

14. **The framework is revisable and does not require every boundary case to have a unique binary verdict.** Difficult cases are part of the subject matter, not defects to be defined away.
