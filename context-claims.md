# Key Claims Index
## Canonical Claims for the Narrow Censorship Essay

This file lists the claims the essay must establish. `outline.md` is authoritative for argument order and detail; this index exists as a compact coherence check.

1. **Censorship is the enforcement of epistemic preference through intervention in an epistemic environment.** It acts by altering conditions of availability, circulation, persistence, visibility, admissibility, standing, attention, or memory.

2. **Censorship is not fundamentally reducible to explicit speech prohibition.** Speech is a major surface, but archives, ranking, access, classification, discoverability, institutional standing, and anticipatory self-restraint can instantiate censorial control.

3. **Censorship is structural rather than actor-defined.** The same operational mode can be instantiated by states, platforms, institutions, communities, economic actors, technical systems, or distributed norms; a single intentional censor is not required.

4. **Not all epistemic influence is censorship.** Persuasion, criticism, rebuttal, disagreement, and ordinary editorial selection remain outside the category unless they function by controlling the practical conditions of epistemic participation rather than contesting a claim on its merits.

5. **Censorship operates through composable operational modes.** The working taxonomy comprises prevention, erasure, suppression, delay, containment, modification, substitution, saturation, delegitimation, inversion, contradiction, and internalisation, with the last three requiring particular care at the boundary with ordinary contestation.

6. **Operational modes must be kept separate from orthogonal analytic axes.** Relevant axes include scale, power distribution, participation in preference formation, persistence/revisability, visibility, justification regime, and domain of control.

7. **Censorship persists partly because complex epistemic systems rely on shortcuts, settlements, classifications, and infrastructures that reduce repeated deliberative and coordination costs.** This is an explanatory claim, not a justification and not a general theory of social control.

8. **Censorship is often infrastructural and low-visibility.** Formal freedom of speech can constrain some modes without eliminating suppression, ranking, delay, containment, classification, standing effects, or internalised restraint.

9. **Classification and evaluation are separate tasks.** Calling a mechanism censorship does not determine whether it is justified, lawful, proportionate, harmful, necessary, democratic, or effective.

10. **The taxonomy is a revisable analytic proposal.** Borderline and poorly fitting cases should remain explicit rather than being forced into the framework.
